context: >-
  An attempted ecosystem decision placed NoCodeHero education strategy and Frontbase/Studygram consumer use cases into
  VEF's vision, roadmap, architecture, and public positioning. That created cross-contamination between a reusable
  framework and independently governed products and businesses.
decision: >-
  VEF owns only the consumer-neutral Vibe Engineering framework for product development. Named consumers, use cases,
  commercial plans, credentials, distribution channels, and product-specific requirements remain canonical in their
  owning repositories. VEF may mention NoCodeHero, Frontbase, Studygram, or another adopter only in decisions, tasks,
  and log when provenance, compatibility work, or a historical boundary requires it; they must not shape or appear in
  VEF's VISION, ROADMAP, ARCHITECTURE, README, index, CLAUDE, or AGENTS. NoCodeHero owns the business-development and
  education plan for two credentials: Certified Vibe Engineer, based on VEF, and Certified No-code Engineer, based on
  the separately governed Frontbase framework.
rationale: >-
  A framework stays reusable only when its product direction is derived from general capabilities rather than one
  founder's portfolio, customer, course, or reference implementation. Consumer needs may inform generic requirements,
  but the framework repository should state the generalized requirement and leave business outcomes and delivery plans
  where they are owned.
consequences: >-
  Positive — clear governance, portable positioning, less roadmap contamination, and autonomous consumer repositories.
  Negative — cross-project coordination cannot be inferred from VEF's public surfaces and must be maintained
  deliberately in each owning repository; compatibility evidence may require narrowly scoped VEF tasks or decisions.
